\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ====================================================================

Legged robots traversing stairs, hurdles, and gaps must perceive
geometry and coordinate vision and control at high rates.  RL
policies trained in simulation have transferred agile locomotion to
hardware~\citep{hwangbo2019learning, lee2020learning, rudin2022learning}.
Recent work~\citep{zhuang2023robot, cheng2023parkour, hoeller2024anymal,
	chane2025soloparkour} extends this to parkour through teacher--student
distillation, where a teacher uses privileged terrain information and
a student imitates it from onboard sensors.

Most such systems assume an onboard depth sensor.  Depth-camera
policies transfer well from simulation~\citep{miki2022learning,
	agarwal2023legged}, but platforms such as the Unitree Go2 ship with
RGB cameras and LiDAR, not a depth camera~\citep{unitreego2}.

Deploying vision policies from RGB therefore requires bridging the \emph{sim-to-real gap}.
Three strategies have emerged: (1)~\textbf{domain randomization}~\citep{tobin2017domain}, which randomizes visual properties; (2)~\textbf{generative augmentation}~\citep{yu2024lucidsim}, which uses diffusion models for photorealistic training images; and (3)~\textbf{canonical representations}, which project both domains into a shared space via a foundation model.

We pursue strategy~(3) using Depth~Anything~V2 (DA2)~\citep{yang2024depth}, a pre-trained monocular depth estimator trained on millions of diverse real images.
If DA2 preserves terrain geometry across visual conditions, then a student trained on estimated depth may inherit some robustness to appearance changes without photorealistic rendering or explicit domain randomization.

\paragraph{Thesis statement.}
We hypothesize that pre-trained monocular depth estimation can provide a useful intermediate representation for vision-based quadruped parkour, enabling a student trained on estimated depth in simulation to tolerate a range of visual conditions without explicit domain randomization.
We evaluate this hypothesis in simulation; real-world transfer remains future work.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
	\item A two-stage teacher--student pipeline from privileged scandot
	      heightmaps to monocular depth for quadruped parkour.
	\item A demonstration that estimated depth enables basic parkour in simulation.
	\item Identification of depth signal quality---governed by model scale and terrain texture---as the key factor limiting performance.
	\item A visual robustness study showing tolerance to lighting and color shifts, but continued sensitivity to texture.
\end{itemize}
