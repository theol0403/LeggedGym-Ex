\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
% ====================================================================

Legged robots traversing stairs, hurdles, and gaps (known as legged parkour) must perceive
geometry and coordinate vision with control at high rates.  RL
policies trained in simulation have transferred agile locomotion to
hardware~\citep{hwangbo2019learning, lee2020learning, rudin2022learning}.
Recent work~\citep{zhuang2023robot, cheng2023parkour, hoeller2024anymal,
	chane2025soloparkour} extends this to parkour through teacher--student
distillation, where a teacher uses privileged terrain information (e.g.\ heightmaps) and
a student imitates it from onboard sensors. However, while such policies using onboard depth cameras transfer well from simulation~\citep{miki2022learning,
	agarwal2023legged}, some platforms including the Unitree Go2 ship with only
RGB cameras and LiDAR~\citep{unitreego2}.

Deploying from RGB instead of depth requires bridging the
\emph{sim-to-real gap}.  Three strategies have emerged:
(1)~\textbf{domain randomization}~\citep{tobin2017domain}, which
randomizes visual properties;
(2)~\textbf{generative augmentation}~\citep{yu2024lucidsim}, which
uses diffusion models for photorealistic training images; and
(3)~\textbf{canonical representations}, which project both domains
into a shared space via a foundation model.

We pursue strategy~(3) using Depth~Anything~V2
(DA2)~\citep{yang2024depth}, a monocular depth estimator trained on
millions of diverse real images.  If DA2 preserves terrain geometry
across visual conditions, a student trained on estimated depth may
tolerate appearance changes without photorealistic rendering or
explicit domain randomization.

\paragraph{Thesis statement.}
We hypothesize that pre-trained monocular depth estimation can serve
as an intermediate representation for vision-based quadruped parkour,
enabling a student policy to tolerate a range of visual conditions
without explicit domain randomization.  We evaluate this hypothesis
in simulation; real-world transfer remains future work.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
	\item A two-stage teacher--student pipeline integrating DA2 into
	      privileged distillation for quadruped parkour.
	\item An empirical demonstration that monocular depth estimation
	      enables parkour in simulation, with model scale and terrain
	      texture identified as key factors.
	\item A visual sensitivity study characterizing when DA2-based
	      policies fail, with Depth Signal Quality (DSQ) as a
	      diagnostic metric.
\end{itemize}
