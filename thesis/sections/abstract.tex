\begin{abstract}
  Vision-based quadruped parkour typically relies on onboard depth cameras, limiting deployment to robots with specialized sensors. We investigate whether a pre-trained \emph{monocular depth estimator} can serve as a visually robust bridge toward sim-to-real transfer from only RGB, without photorealistic rendering or domain randomization.

  We present a two-phase pipeline: a privileged teacher trained via PPO with ground-truth terrain scans, distilled into a visual student via DAgger operating on depth images from Depth~Anything~V2 (DA2).
  In simulation, we demonstrate that this pipeline achieves limited parkour capabilities under conditions characterized by a \emph{Depth Signal Quality} (DSQ) metric.
  Domain invariance experiments show that DA2-based students are robust to lighting and color perturbations, and generalize across simulated environments.
  All results are in simulation; real-world transfer remains to be validated.
\end{abstract}
