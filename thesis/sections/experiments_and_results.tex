\section{Experiments and Results}
\label{sec:experiments}
% ====================================================================

We evaluate the DA2 pipeline through three experiments:
(1)~establishing upper-bound baselines with ground-truth sensing,
(2)~closing the gap between estimated and ground-truth depth by
varying model capacity and terrain texture, and
(3)~testing domain invariance under visual perturbations.
We report \emph{success rate} (fraction of episodes completing the
full course) and \emph{progress ratio} (mean fractional distance
along the course before termination), both averaged over 256
episodes.  

When analyzing visual robustness, we also report
\emph{depth signal quality} (DSQ), defined as the scale- and
offset-invariant Pearson correlation
between ground-truth and DA2-estimated depth images, averaged over
environments and time steps. 

\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{DSQ} = \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^{T}
    \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N}
      \frac{
        \sum_p (d^{\mathrm{GT}}_{i,p} - \bar{d}^{\mathrm{GT}}_i)
              (d^{\mathrm{DA2}}_{i,p} - \bar{d}^{\mathrm{DA2}}_i)
      }{
        \| d^{\mathrm{GT}}_i - \bar{d}^{\mathrm{GT}}_i \|_2 \;
        \| d^{\mathrm{DA2}}_i - \bar{d}^{\mathrm{DA2}}_i \|_2
      }
  \label{eq:dsq}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Baselines}
\label{sec:baselines}

We start by establishing upper bounds for the privileged teacher and the ground-truth depth sensing student (GT-depth).
The privileged teacher achieves near-perfect success across all gap difficulties, shown in Table~\ref{tab:baselines}.

The GT-depth student, receiving simulator depth images,
achieves success on all rows, confirming the CNN--GRU
architecture extracts sufficient geometry from a single depth viewpoint.
A zero-depth ablation (all depth pixels set to zero)
drops success to 8\%, verifying the policy relies on depth
rather than exploiting proprioceptive heuristics.

\subsection{Parkour from Estimated Depth}
\label{sec:da2_results}

We now determine whether parkour can be achieved from estimated
depth. Starting from the GT-depth setup, we replace simulator
depth with DA2-small. 
As shown in Table~\ref{tab:da2_main}, 
DA2-small achieves success on early rows, but fails completely on Rows~2 and 3.
Switching to DA2-base results in promotion to Row 3, but it still falls well short of
GT-depth on the hardest rows.

The remaining limitation is the visual input. On untextured terrain,
the gap boundary provides weak cues, so the depth estimate is
ambiguous. As shown in Table~\ref{tab:da2_main},
adding a checkerboard terrain texture makes the gap geometry
visible and closes most of the remaining gap to GT-depth.
Figure~\ref{fig:da2_texture_comparison} shows examples of depth estimation
directly across representative surface conditions.

\subsection{Domain Invariance}
\label{sec:domain}

We now test whether DA2 provides invariance to the visual domain shifts encountered in sim-to-real transfer by
perturbing the trained student only at \emph{test time}.  
Table~\ref{tab:domain_inv}
reports success, progress, and depth signal quality for texture replacement and lighting/color changes, while
Figure~\ref{fig:dsq_scatter} plots DSQ against policy success.

\begin{figure}[h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{figures/dsq_scatter.pdf}
  \caption{Success rate vs. DSQ. Featureless surfaces (red) cluster

Lighting and color shifts cause only modest degradation, indicating
that DA2 is largely robust to photometric variation.  Texture shifts
are more mixed: concrete, stone tiles, gravel, and noise remain
strong, while wood and bricks reduce success substantially despite
high DSQ.  Featureless surfaces are the only catastrophic failure
mode, collapsing both DSQ and success.  DSQ is therefore a useful
failure indicator for missing geometric signal, but high DSQ alone
does not guarantee full robustness across all unseen textures.
