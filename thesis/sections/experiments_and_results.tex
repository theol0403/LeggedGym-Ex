\section{Experiments and Results}
\label{sec:experiments}
% ====================================================================

We evaluate the DA2 pipeline through three experiments:
(1)~establishing upper-bound baselines with ground-truth sensing,
(2)~closing the gap between estimated and ground-truth depth by
varying model capacity and terrain texture, and
(3)~testing visual sensitivity under appearance perturbations.
We report \emph{success rate} (fraction of episodes completing the
full course) and \emph{progress ratio} (mean fractional distance
along the course before termination), both averaged over 256
episodes.  For visual sensitivity, we also report DSQ
(Section~\ref{sec:dsq_math}).

% ====================================================================
\subsection{Baselines}
\label{sec:baselines}

We first establish upper bounds with the privileged teacher and
a ground-truth depth student (GT-depth).
Table~\ref{tab:baselines} shows near-perfect teacher performance
across all gap difficulties.
The GT-depth student also succeeds on all rows, confirming that the
CNN--GRU architecture can act from a single depth view.  A
zero-depth ablation drops success to 8\%, confirming the policy
relies on depth rather than proprioceptive heuristics.

\subsection{Parkour from Estimated Depth}
\label{sec:da2_results}

We replace simulator depth with DA2 estimates.  As
Table~\ref{tab:da2_main} shows, DA2-small succeeds
on early rows but fails on Rows~2--3.  DA2-base reaches Row~3 but
falls well short of GT-depth on harder rows.

On untextured terrain, the gap boundary provides weak cues, so the depth estimate is
ambiguous. As Table~\ref{tab:da2_main} shows, adding a checkerboard
texture makes the gap geometry visible and closes most of the
remaining gap to GT-depth. Figure~\ref{fig:da2_texture_comparison}
shows representative depth estimates across surface conditions.

\subsection{Visual Sensitivity}
\label{sec:domain}

To test appearance sensitivity, we perturb the trained student at
\emph{test time only} in simulation.  Table~\ref{tab:domain_inv}
reports success across six terrain configurations (three obstacle
families $\times$ two difficulty levels) under texture replacement,
lighting/color changes, and combined perturbations.
Figure~\ref{fig:dsq_scatter} plots DSQ against policy success.

On Row 0, stairs and hurdles are largely insensitive to appearance
changes because strong edges remain visible across textures and
lighting.  Gaps are more sensitive because their boundary is visible
mainly through surface texture. On Row 3, all three obstacle families degrade, including
stairs and hurdles.  Featureless surfaces cause the largest failures,
while lighting and color shifts have a smaller effect.  This pattern
suggests DA2 is robust to photometric variation but still depends on
texture when obstacle boundaries are weak.

As Figure~\ref{fig:dsq_scatter} shows, DSQ is a useful failure
indicator: featureless surfaces collapse both DSQ and success.
However, high DSQ alone does not guarantee robustness, since some
unseen textures still reduce success despite preserving coarse depth
structure.
