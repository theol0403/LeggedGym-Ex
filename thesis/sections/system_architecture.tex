\section{System Architecture}
\label{sec:arch}
% ====================================================================

\subsection{Setup}

All experiments use the Genesis GPU
simulator~\cite{genesis2024} with the
legged\_gym framework~\cite{rudin2022learning} and
rsl\_rl~\cite{schwarke2025rslrl} on 4$\times$ NVIDIA L40S GPUs.
The robot is a Unitree Go2 quadruped with 12 actuated joints.
Actions $a_t \in \R^{12}$ are target joint positions, updated at 50Hz and converted to torques via a PD controller.

\subsection{Teacher Network}

The teacher processes observations through three parallel MLP
encoders---scandot ($\R^{132} \to \R^{32}$), privileged environment
parameters ($\to \R^{32}$), and a 1D convolution over proprioceptive
history---whose outputs are concatenated with proprioception and
fed into the actor MLP ($\to \R^{12}$ joint targets).

\subsection{Student Network}

The student replaces the scandot encoder with a recurrent depth
encoder (Figure~\ref{fig:student_arch}).
\label{sec:depth_norm}
DA2's metric depth output is first normalized to $[-0.5, 0.5]$
via online EMA percentile statistics.
A CNN backbone reduces the depth image to a 32-dim feature vector,
which is concatenated with proprioception (heading commands masked
to zero), passed through a combination MLP, and fed into a
single-layer GRU (hidden dim 512) that provides temporal context.

The GRU output produces a 32-dim terrain latent $z$ and a 2-dim
heading prediction $\hat{y}$.  The actor MLP takes
$[o_\text{prop}; z]$ and outputs 12 joint targets, sharing the same
architecture as the teacher actor.

\paragraph{RGB baseline.}
\label{sec:resnet}
To compare DA2 depth with direct RGB, we train a student that
replaces the depth CNN with a frozen
ResNet-18~\cite{he2016deep} truncated at \texttt{layer2}, producing
a 32-dim feature via adaptive pooling and a trainable projection.
The rest of the architecture (GRU, actor) is identical.

\subsection{Terrain and Curriculum}

The simulator generates parkour terrains with three obstacle
families (stairs, hurdles, gaps) at four difficulty levels.
Gap widths range from 0.24\,m (Row~0) to 0.50\,m (Row~3).  During
teacher training, a curriculum advances robots to harder rows upon
success.
